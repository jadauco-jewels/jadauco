Imitation jewellery lasts far longer than most people expect, and the difference between a set
that looks new after three years and one that looks tired after three months is almost entirely
in how it is stored.

## The one rule that matters

**Last on, first off.** Put your jewellery on after perfume, hairspray, moisturiser and makeup,
and take it off before you take off anything else. Alcohol and oils are what strip a gold
polish, and they are in almost every cosmetic.

## Storage

- Keep each piece in its own pouch or a lined box compartment. Pieces rubbing against each other
  is the second biggest cause of wear after cosmetics.
- Add a silica gel sachet to the box — the ones that come in shoe boxes work perfectly. Humidity
  is what tarnishes brass.
- Never store jewellery in the bathroom, however convenient it is.

## Cleaning

Wipe with a dry, soft cotton cloth after every wear. That is genuinely all most pieces need.

For stone work that has dulled, use a **dry** soft toothbrush to lift dust out of the settings.
Do not use water, soap, toothpaste, lemon or any commercial silver cleaner on plated jewellery —
they all remove the polish, some of them immediately.

## What to keep it away from

Water, sweat, perfume, hairspray, chlorine, sea water, and household cleaning products. Take
pieces off before swimming, before the gym, and before you sleep.

## How long a polish lasts

With the care above, expect two to four years of regular wear before a gold polish starts to
thin at the contact points — clasp edges, the underside of a ring, the back of a bangle. Worn
daily without care, expect closer to six months. Neither is a defect; plating is a coating and
coatings wear.

If a piece is important to you and the polish has gone, most local jewellers will re-plate
brass for a modest fee.
